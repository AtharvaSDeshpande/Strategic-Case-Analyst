# Panel Notes

## IKEA
**Core Capability:** Cost Engineering (Democratic Design)

**1. Porter's Value Chain:**
- **Fit:** Fits perfectly because IKEA's entire model is about reconfiguring traditional value chain activities. They offload outbound logistics and assembly to the customer, and tightly integrate inbound logistics with design (flat-packing).
- **Anecdote:** Designing products backwards from a target price, resulting in the iconic flat-pack that fits perfectly on standard pallets and in customer cars.
- **Ledger IDs:** IKE-01, IKE-02

**2. Corporate Scope:**
- **Fit:** Fits because IKEA has historically maintained a tight corporate scope, focusing mostly on home furnishings and related services (like in-store restaurants to keep shoppers engaged).
- **Anecdote:** Expanding vertically into forestry and manufacturing to secure supply chains, rather than expanding horizontally into unrelated retail.
- **Ledger IDs:** IKE-01

## NVIDIA
**Core Capability:** Parallel Computing Architecture & Ecosystem (CUDA)

**3. Porter's 5 Forces:**
- **Fit:** The AI boom highlights the immense barriers to entry and high switching costs created by the CUDA software ecosystem, neutralizing substitute threats and locking in buyers.
- **Anecdote:** Researchers attempting to migrate established AI models face crippling costs to rewrite kernels, keeping them locked into NVIDIA hardware even when cheaper alternatives exist.
- **Ledger IDs:** NVI-01, NVI-02

**4. Aghion-Howitt (Schumpeterian Creative Destruction / Growth):**
- **Fit:** NVIDIA cannibalizes its own products through rapid innovation cycles. Each new GPU generation renders the previous obsolete, driving continuous growth through self-disruption.
- **Anecdote:** Releasing new architectures every two years that make their own older chips seem inefficient, ensuring they destroy their own legacy market before competitors can.
- **Ledger IDs:** NVI-01

## Unilever
**Core Capability:** Brand Portfolio Management at Scale

**5. BCG Matrix:**
- **Fit:** A classic application. Unilever manages a portfolio of hundreds of brands, constantly assessing which ones are "cash cows" (like Hellmann's) and which are "dogs" or "question marks", leading to rationalization.
- **Anecdote:** Pruning the portfolio from 1,600 brands down to 400 core brands to focus capital on high-growth, high-margin categories.
- **Ledger IDs:** UNI-01, UNI-02

**6. Ansoff Matrix:**
- **Fit:** Unilever grows through market penetration (pushing existing brands deeper into emerging markets) and product development (introducing sustainable or premium variants).
- **Anecdote:** Shifting towards Prestige Beauty and Health & Wellbeing to capture new market segments with higher margins.
- **Ledger IDs:** UNI-02
