# Ansoff Matrix

Accumulated guidance on mapping and risk-rating growth moves.

## How to apply this well

- Use it as a **risk map of moves already made**, not as a menu of options. Where a firm's moves
  cluster tells you how much unfamiliar capability it is currently absorbing.
- Apply a two-question test per move: does the firm still sell to a buyer it knows, using a
  capability it has? Two negatives is diversification risk however the move is branded.
- **Market development fails on go-to-market more often than on product.** Reaching an unfamiliar
  buyer usually requires a different sales structure, and firms that change the target without
  changing the structure tend to stall. Check whether the structure moved.
- Map **rivals on the same grid**. A quadrant a competitor occupies and the subject does not is
  usually the sharpest finding the framework produces.
- Watch for **one asset counted twice**. When a single investment produces two apparent moves,
  scoring both as independent strengths inflates the assessment.
- State the **buyer unit** you assumed. The same move is product development or market development
  depending on whether the buyer is the same person in a new guise or a genuinely different buyer,
  and the risk grade changes with it.

## Signals that the application is weak

- Quadrants described generically rather than populated with this firm's actual moves.
- A firm's own framing of a move accepted without testing it.
