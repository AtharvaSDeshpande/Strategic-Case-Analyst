# Generic Strategies

Accumulated guidance on placing a firm's competitive position.

## How to apply this well

- Apply a **two-limb test** before calling anything cost leadership. First, the firm charges less
  than rivals *and* earns an equal or better margin; a lower price on a lower margin is discounting.
  Second, the advantage traces to a named activity that is physically done differently and widens
  with volume. A firm that passes only the second limb has a structural difference that has not yet
  become a cost position, and should be described that way.
- A **narrow focus is often a stage rather than a destination**. Extreme standardisation inside a
  narrow segment is a common route to the scale that later supports a broad cost position.
- The **intended position and the realised position frequently differ**. An analyst reading only
  end-state numbers will over-attribute the outcome to a deliberate plan, because the design account
  is the more available story and usually the only one the sources support. State both readings and
  say which the evidence can settle.
- Reserve the stuck-in-the-middle diagnosis for genuine evidence of an unresolved trade-off: middling
  price *and* middling margin. Doing two things is not the same as being stuck between them.
- Plot rivals alongside the subject. A single point on a positioning grid asserts a position; several
  points demonstrate one.

## Signals that the application is weak

- The largest firm labelled the cost leader with no margin comparison.
- A position taken from the firm's own strategy statement rather than from its results.
