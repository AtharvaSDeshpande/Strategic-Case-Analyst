# Blue Ocean Strategy

Accumulated guidance on testing claims of uncontested market space.

## How to apply this well

- Apply a **strict structural test**: genuine new space removes a cost element from the structure
  *and* creates a buyer value element the industry has never offered. A new price point, a new
  segment or a new marketing angle is competition inside the existing boundary.
- Use the **ownership tell**. If two or more incumbents can enter the supposed new space using
  assets they already own, it was an adjacency. The first mover has a timing and capacity lead
  inside a contested area, which is real and different, and should be named as such.
- Separate the **category claim from the firm claim**. A category can be genuinely new while no
  individual firm's position in it is uncontested. Both can be true at once and conflating them
  overstates the firm.
- Insist on **unit economics** before calling something value innovation. Without cost to serve,
  price realisation and retention for the new activity, subsidised capacity expansion and genuine
  value innovation are indistinguishable from outside. Where those figures are not disclosed, report
  that the evidence cannot settle it.
- Remember that uncontested space becomes contested. The useful question is how long the window is
  and what the firm does with it.
- Firms often reach new space by extending an existing capability into a segment just outside their
  usual boundary, so this framework and the adjacency grid are frequently two readings of the same
  move.

## Signals that the application is weak

- A novel product treated as sufficient evidence, with no change in the cost structure shown.
- The four-actions questions answered with adjectives rather than with named industry factors.
