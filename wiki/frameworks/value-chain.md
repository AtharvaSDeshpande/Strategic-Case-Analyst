# Value Chain

Accumulated guidance on decomposing a firm into activities and locating the advantage.

## How to apply this well

- Genuine cost advantage comes from **structurally redesigning** an activity or removing it from the
  chain, not from performing the industry's standard activities slightly more cheaply. Test for the
  redesign before accepting a cost-leadership claim.
- Name the **linkages**, not only the activities. The most defensible advantages usually sit in how
  two activities interact, because a linkage is harder to observe and harder to copy piecemeal.
- A **support activity can be the primary strategic driver**. Where the product is really a service
  or an environment, the activity that recruits, trains and rewards people may be what creates the
  value, with the primary activities merely delivering it. Do not assume the advantage lives among
  the primary activities.
- Extreme **vertical integration can be a rational response to missing external markets** rather
  than empire-building. Judge how much of the chain a firm owns against what its environment can
  actually supply.
- Encode **evidence** in the exhibit. Mark activities the sources do not disclose as undisclosed
  rather than describing them generically. A chain with every box filled overstates what is known,
  and procurement, input costs and service quality are undisclosed more often than not.

## Signals that the application is weak

- Every box populated, with no activity marked unknown.
- Activities described but no linkage identified.
- A chain that would look identical for any firm in the same industry.


## 2026-09-24
- **Application Learning**: Value Chain analysis isn't just about finding where costs are added; it's about finding where time can be eliminated. A structural advantage is created when a step (like storage) is entirely removed rather than just optimized.


## 2026-09-24 - Retail Reconfiguration
- Taught that cost engineering isn't just a support activity, but a constraint that dictates the entire inbound and outbound logistics configuration (e.g., forcing customer co-production).
