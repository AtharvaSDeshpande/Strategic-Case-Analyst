# Stakeholder Map

Accumulated guidance on the power and interest grid.

## How to apply this well

- The grid earns its place when it is **dynamic**. Current positions are description; the finding is
  which stakeholder is about to move between quadrants, and what triggers the move. Implementation
  risk concentrates at relocations.
- Treating a group as low-power is usually a **choice, not a structural fact**. Deliberately
  elevating a group's power, through pay, equity, information or representation, is a legitimate
  strategic instrument and can be the mechanism that makes a strategy work.
- Name **stakeholders who do not exist yet** but will. Incoming investors after a listing, or a
  regulator about to acquire a new power, carry no weight today and may dominate tomorrow. Their
  arrival also demotes whoever currently holds unconstrained control.
- Distinguish **power over the firm from interest in it**. A party with high interest and no power
  is a communication problem; a party with high power and low interest is a risk that activates
  without warning.
- Check whether an internal group's position matches what the strategy needs it to be. Where the
  customer promise depends on frontline staff, a grid that places those staff in a low-power
  quadrant has found the misalignment.

## Signals that the application is weak

- A static map with no expected movement.
- Stakeholders listed by category rather than by the specific power they hold.
