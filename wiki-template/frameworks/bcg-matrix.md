# Growth-Share Matrix

Accumulated guidance on portfolio and capital-allocation analysis.

## How to apply this well

- Read the portfolio as a **pipeline**, not a snapshot. Mature high-share businesses fund the
  high-growth low-share ones, which become leaders, which mature in turn. A portfolio assessed
  statically misses the mechanism the framework exists to expose.
- A cash-generating business **is only useful if there is something to feed**. A portfolio of mature
  leaders with no growth candidates is not a strong portfolio, it is a liquidating one.
- In environments where external capital is scarce or expensive, a diversified group can act as its
  **own internal capital market**, funding a large build-out from a mature business rather than from
  outside investors. Judge that against what the external market could actually have supplied.
- **Relative share means relative to the largest rival.** Plotting absolute share quietly changes
  what the matrix says.
- State what **bubble area** encodes, normally revenue or capital employed. The convention only
  works if the third variable is declared.
- Do not use it to compare **rivals inside one industry**. It is built for units facing different
  industries; with a single industry the attractiveness axis is constant and the exhibit collapses to
  one dimension. Use a weighted competitive profile matrix instead.
- Every plotted position and size must come from a disclosed figure. An estimated bubble is
  indistinguishable from a measured one once drawn.

## Signals that the application is weak

- Quadrant assignments with no two sourced numbers behind them.
- No statement of which unit funds which.
