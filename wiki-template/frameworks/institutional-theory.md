# Institutional Theory

Accumulated guidance on reading strategy against the rules of the game.

## How to apply this well

- **Check which voids are actually open now, before applying void logic.** A functioning regulator
  publishing unit-level data, a working route to public capital and institutional investors buying
  in directly are the opposite of institutional voids. The framework over-predicts badly when a
  model built for one era is applied to an environment whose relevant gaps have closed.
- Group affiliation on its own buys nothing. What shows up in results is a parent **willing to
  concentrate** capital in one business. Affiliated firms starved of capital are the control case
  that disproves the naive version of the premium argument.
- Where voids are genuinely wide, expect **internalisation**: own capital allocation, own talent
  pipeline, own logistics. That also predicts an unusually integrated value chain, so the two
  frameworks should agree.
- Watch for the **political solvency variable**. Where an obligation of unusual scale sits outside
  the banking system, its resolution may be an administrative decision rather than a market one,
  which makes a competitor's survival partly a policy question. No industry-structure framework
  captures that.
- Do not claim a **cross-subsidy you cannot trace**. Group-level capital expenditure is not evidence
  of a transfer between units.
- The premium and the discount are the same mechanism at different stages of institutional maturity,
  so date your assessment and say what would flip it.

## Signals that the application is weak

- Voids asserted as a general property of a region rather than demonstrated as currently open.
- A conglomerate judged inefficient purely against a mature-market benchmark.
