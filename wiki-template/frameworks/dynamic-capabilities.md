# Dynamic Capabilities

Accumulated guidance on assessing whether a firm can keep renewing its advantage.

## How to apply this well

- Rate **sensing, seizing and transforming separately**, each as strong, partial or absent. Sensing
  and seizing are comparatively easy to evidence. Transforming is the one firms announce and do not
  finish, and grading the three together hides exactly that gap.
- Distinguish **financial reconfiguration from organisational reconfiguration**. A rebuilt balance
  sheet is genuine transforming and is not the same as a changed operating model. Where only the
  financial kind is evidenced, say so rather than claiming the capability in general.
- **Announced, filed and completed are three different states.** Report which one the evidence
  supports as at the source date.
- This is the mechanism that **repairs the organisational fit** that static resource tests check at
  a point in time. Where a resource test returns "organised: no", ask whether the transforming
  cluster is capable of fixing it.
- Do not claim **ambidexterity without exploration outcomes**. Reach and audience figures are
  activity measures. Without revenue, margin or retention attributable to the new venture, the
  capability can be neither claimed nor denied, and reporting that is the correct finding.
- Use a **control case**: a rival that senses correctly but cannot seize because of its balance sheet
  shows that seizing capacity is largely a funding question.
- **Separate mistiming from incapacity.** Falling returns during a heavy investment phase are
  ordinary. Before diagnosing a capability gap, ask whether the asset base has simply not reached
  steady-state use, and say honestly whether the available observations can settle it.

## Signals that the application is weak

- A narrative of adaptation treated as evidence of capability.
- Any acquisition treated as seizing, with no business-model change shown.
